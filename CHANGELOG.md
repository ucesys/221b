# Changelog

What changed in each release of 221B, for people who use it and people who install it. Versions follow [semantic versioning](https://semver.org/): while the major version is 0, a minor version (0.7.0) may change behaviour and chart values, and a patch version (0.7.1) only fixes. [docs/upgrading.md](docs/upgrading.md) says how to upgrade and what each upgrade changes.

## 0.12.0 (2026-11-11)

Phase 12: AI agents, the AIOps area (cases, jobs, proposed changes and Mycroft's knowledge), hosts outside Kubernetes, backups to S3 and Helm repositories, with the fixes of the Phase 12 review (`docs/phase-12-review.md`). Requirement rows before CF-91 call the AIOps area *Cases*.

### Upgrading

- **New records, nothing to migrate from 0.11.** The chart makes the ConfigMaps `<release>-ai`, `<release>-agents`, `<release>-issues`, `<release>-proposals`, `<release>-runs`, `<release>-knowledge`, `<release>-wiki` and `<release>-casework`, and each case is a ConfigMap of its own, `<release>-case-<n>`, so the console's Role may now create, list and delete ConfigMaps in its namespace. The console makes the Secrets it needs (`<release>-backup-key` at its first start, the others when a credential is first saved). Every job is off until a SuperAdmin turns it on. New chart values are listed in [docs/upgrading.md](docs/upgrading.md#0120-2026-11-11).
- **Builds of 0.12.0 before the AIOps rename** (CF-104, ADR-152) kept the proposed changes in `<release>-cases`: at its first start the console copies them into `<release>-proposals` once, never over what the new ConfigMap holds, and logs it; the chart still makes `<release>-cases` so that the upgrade does not delete it first. The browser app's API moved from `/api/v1/cases/…` to `/api/v1/aiops/…`, with no alias; the issue API, `/api/issues/v1/…`, keeps its paths. Links of before, `#/cases/…`, open their place under `#/aiops/…`.
- **Builds of 0.12.0 before the one case flow** (wave 6) are not converted: set `cases.cleanStart.token` once to clear their AIOps records (CF-23, ADR-130). An upgrade from 0.11 has nothing to clean.

### Added

#### AI agents

- **Nine agents, with Sherlock at the front desk** (AI-01 to AI-07, AI-15 to AI-18, ADR-74 to ADR-79): the Troubleshooter, the Change planner, the Auditor, the Host doctor, the Automation author, the Script reviewer, Mycroft and the Summarizer, each with one purpose, its own provider and model, daily and monthly caps, clusters and only its purpose's tools, set in Administration › Settings › AI › Agents. Sherlock hands parts of a question to them (`hand_over`, at most three deep), and the answer shows each as *Asked the Troubleshooter*, opening to what came back, its model and cost (AI-21). Every agent has `ask_knowledge`; every agent call is audited (`ai.agent`). The README lists the agents (AS-09).
- **Providers** (AI-01, AS-04 to AS-06): any number, Anthropic, OpenAI-compatible, Amazon Bedrock or Google Vertex AI, each with its credential in a Secret of its own and a *Test* in its dialog, before and after saving. A provider that answers without a key (an in-house vLLM or Ollama) is usable once its test passed (*No key needed*). The model is picked from the endpoint's list, with its context window.
- **Remove any provider, and make any the default** (AI-22, KI-2): *Remove* is on every provider, the default's too, and removes it once no agent uses it; when agents still use it, it says which. *Make default* moves the agents that name no provider to another one.
- **Instructions in layers, from draft to live** (AI-08 to AI-14, AS-01 to AS-03): each agent's built-in text, shown in full and editable with *Reset to built-in* and *Compare with built-in*; house instructions (the chart's `agents.<agent>.instructions`, or edited in the console: *In sync*, *Edited in app*, *Baseline changed*); notes for every cluster and per cluster. An edit goes *Draft* → *Test* (recorded test cases and replays of real answers, cost shown first) → *Pilot* (chosen people or clusters) → *Live* (a second SuperAdmin's approval), each step a button that says why it waits; every live version restores in one click; any answer can be saved as a test case.
- **Sherlock's compact form** (AS-07) for a model whose context its whole form does not fit: about 2,500 tokens of instructions and tools instead of about 10,000; the agent's drawer says how much of the context it takes.
- **Show costs** (CF-40): a SuperAdmin switch in Settings › AI › Agents; off, no dollar amount shows anywhere but the budget fields.
- **Prompt sources** (GS-13): [docs/prompt-sources.md](docs/prompt-sources.md) records what the agents' instructions drew on; `hack/check-prompt-sources.sh` fails on a source it does not record.

#### The AIOps area

- **AIOps, a third area** beside Kubernetes and Automation (CF-91, CF-104, ADR-88, ADR-152), at `#/aiops/…`, across clusters, shown to whoever has something in it: **Inbox**; **Jobs** (Schedule, Runs, Validation rules); **Mycroft** (Wiki, Known issues, Exceptions, Fixes). Sherlock and Watson are in the dock on every page, and Sherlock knows the area's pages, buttons and who may use them, and the item open (its AIOps skill, IB-10 to IB-13, IN-10). Settings has an *AIOps* group: Issues, SLA, Cases and Jobs.
- **The Inbox, one table** (IN-01 to IN-06, CF-17, CF-35 to CF-38, CF-66, CF-81, ADR-116): every case, proposed change, approval, known issue draft and knowledge draft (and, for a SuperAdmin, suggested validation rules, CF-87), pending first, then P0 first, then the longest waiting, each with its priority, type, incident number, cluster, object (linked, with its age), status, waiting time, assignee and *Last change* ("Auditor (agent) · 2h ago"). Filters in the address: Pending or All, People or Agents, Mine or Unassigned, priority, type, status, cluster and a search that finds past RCAs by their cause; *late* names the SLA target passed. Pick many cases and assign, prioritize, analyze or close them at once. A row opens its drawer at `#/aiops/inbox/<kind>/<id>`; *N alerts now* links the cluster's Alerts tab.
- **The bell** (CF-80, IN-07), right of *Live* in every area: red while a P0 or P1 case waits for you, yellow while anything else does; it opens the Inbox. The Inbox tab counts what waits.
- **Every problem is a case** (CF-01 to CF-05, CF-07, CF-14 to CF-16, CF-45, CF-67, CF-90, ADR-126 to ADR-133): a person's report, *New case*, an alert or an agent's finding, numbered `C-<n>`. A case is *New*, *In progress* (only a person puts it there; asking the reporter shows *Waiting for the reporter*) or *Closed* with one outcome: *Fixed* (an RCA, required at P0 and P1), *Duplicate* (of an open original), *No action needed*, *Known issue* or *Exception* (agent-found cases). A finding that repeats an open case joins it (*seen ×N*), is recorded on its known issue (*still present*), is dropped by an exception, reopens a fix that did not hold, or opens a regression. Priorities run P0 (outage), P1 (blocking), P2 (service degradation), P3 (preemptive) to P4 (question); agent-found cases have an SLA only at P0 and P1. The states are drawn in [docs/states.md](docs/states.md).
- **New case** (CF-12, CU-02, CU-03, CF-36, CF-37): a title, what you know, a cluster or none, what it is about in any words, a priority, a type and an incident number; as you type it shows the open cases, known issues, exceptions and recent closes like it, with their RCAs. An alert's *New case* opens a case about its object.
- **The case drawer** (CF-18, CF-46 to CF-49, CF-58 to CF-60, CF-70): near full screen, the case on the left and Sherlock and Watson on the right (tabs on a phone). One Sherlock conversation per case, shared by the people who work it and naming who asked, deleted when the case closes. Watson's commands from the case are logged on it, and the changes that worked start *What was done* when it is fixed. Priority, type and assignee in the header, the other fields edited in place (a pencil on each), the cluster by name, and one shared draft with *Save*, which asks before overwriting someone's newer edit.
- **Case analysis** (CW-04, CW-06, CF-32, CF-43, CF-49, CF-52, CF-82, CF-83): the Troubleshooter, read only, gives the cause, the evidence, a suggested fix with *Run in Watson* on each command, and proposed changes. It starts from the earlier cases like it with their RCAs, the open ones, the known issues it matches and the active fixes. New agent-found cases at P0 to P2 are analyzed by themselves, one at a time within the job's budget; *Analyze* and *Analyze again* run it at once. When it finds no problem it says why, and a case an agent or the console opened at P0 to P2 goes down to P3 unless a person set its priority.
- **Proposed changes** (WC-01 to WC-19, CF-25 to CF-31, CF-34, CF-39, CF-56, CF-61, CF-62, CF-92, IB-08, ADR-99 to ADR-102): every change an agent wants, in a chat or on its own, is a proposed change `PC-<n>` that waits in the Inbox and the cluster's Watson panel and outlives a restart: what started it, the agent and model, why, the evidence, one line per step (*What Watson will run*, *What it changes in the console*) with impact and risk, the cases and known issues it resolves, and its expiry (24 hours, or when its alert resolves). Its drawer opens like a case's, with a Sherlock conversation of its own. *Run in Watson* brings it to Watson, where the one approval is given after a re-check, by those in `cases.approverRoles` (two different people on production); *Reject…* asks why. Applied, it records the fix on each case it resolves; closing a case rejects the changes waiting only for it.
- **A fix is verified only on what a run re-checked** (CF-29, CF-30, CF-06, CF-94 to CF-96, ADR-145): the nightly audit and the alert diagnosis are given their job's waiting fixes inside what they read and say of each whether the problem is still there; only *gone* verifies it, and then a case below P1 closes as *Fixed* by itself, while at P0 and P1 it waits for its RCA. The case says which run verified it and what it read, or how long it has waited and how many runs passed. A fix re-checked and still there did not hold.
- **Fixes** (CF-50 to CF-55): closing a case as *Fixed* with *Save fix* (on by default) saves its fix in AIOps › Mycroft › Fixes, one per problem with the cases it fixed, which people edit and which outlive their cases; the agents are given the active ones.
- **Exceptions** (CF-13, CF-68, CF-69, CF-76): AIOps › Mycroft › Exceptions lists them, each covering one cluster, object and validation rule, with its justification, matches and last match; *New exception* suggests the cluster's objects as you type; Active, Stale, Expired, Inactive and All, with *Make active again*. Exceptions are the console's records, given to the agents in their task.
- **Known issues** (CS-13, CS-14, CF-14, CF-63, JR-10): AIOps › Mycroft › Known issues, with the editor (symptoms in users' words, services, status, workaround, expected fix, owner), dated updates, *Accept the proposal* on an agent's draft, Follow, and how often each is still present.
- **Validation rules** (CF-08, CF-71 to CF-75, CF-85 to CF-87, CF-89, CF-93, ADR-138): the ids the agents key their findings by, each with its meaning, in AIOps › Jobs › Validation rules, a SuperAdmin's: one table of the rules, the ids agents made up (*Unlisted*, with the agents' description) and suggestions, to add, edit, retire, replace by another and promote. The daily Housekeeping suggests rules and promotes only exact repeats; a suggestion waits in a SuperAdmin's Inbox.
- **Housekeeping** (CF-53, CF-54, CF-75, CF-100): a daily job with no agent that makes duplicate fixes, known issues and exceptions inactive, marks contradicting fixes pending, suggests validation rules, offers proven fixes to Mycroft, and puts right a case whose work was not saved with it; it reports what it did. The fixes of cases closed as Fixed before were imported once.
- **Settings › Cases and Settings › SLA** (CF-19, CU-06, CF-78): how long closed agent-found cases are kept (90 days), New case's look-back, when an exception is stale, the caps per run, and after how many minutes a critical alert nobody handled opens a case; the first-response and resolution targets per priority.
- **Alerts and cases** (CF-77 to CF-79, CF-82, CF-84): an alert's row links the open case that covers it, in place of *New case*; an alert diagnosis that concluded acknowledges its alert in the job's name, with a link to its run and case; a critical alert still going after 15 minutes that no case covers and no run concluded on opens a P0 case by itself; a case from an alert is named by its reason (*CrashLoopBackOff: back-off restarting failed container: shop/Pod/web-1*).

#### Jobs

- **Agents on their own** (JB-01 to JB-22, JR-05 to JR-07, CF-103, ADR-90 to ADR-93): AIOps › Jobs › Schedule lists the jobs, each with its agent (*Nightly audit · Auditor*): the *Nightly audit*, the *Alert diagnosis* of each new alert (a repeat updates its run), the *Daily digest*, the *Case analysis*, the *Case triage* of each reported case, *Housekeeping*, the *Drift check*, a *Wiki sync* per knowledge source and *Backup to S3*. Each is off until a SuperAdmin turns it on, with its clusters, a schedule (read back in words, in a time zone) or an event, a budget per run and per day, its lowest priority (P3 by default, CF-33), and a name and instructions of its own (*Reset to built-in*); *Run now*, *Pause* and *Add another <kind> job* are in its drawer. Schedules run once, on the leading replica; every change and run is audited.
- **Agents on their own read as `221b:agents`** (JB-08), never as a person, with the role Settings › Jobs chooses: Viewer (the default) or SuperViewer. Settings › Jobs also says who may edit jobs besides SuperAdmins. Nothing a job does changes anything: a change it wants waits as a proposed change.
- **Runs** (JB-14, JB-16, JR-01 to JR-04, CF-11, CF-95, CF-97 to CF-99, CF-101, FX-12): AIOps › Jobs › Runs lists every run with when it started and ended, its health in the tone of its worst finding (*Done · 2 critical*), what each finding did (opened, seen, known, excepted, reopened, regression, ignored, expected) with a link, the fixes it verified, its proposals, tokens and cost; a run under way can be cancelled. A run says what it could not be used for: a cluster that did not answer (*Done, with errors*), the findings left out past its 40, the fixes it did not re-check, and findings not routed while the exceptions could not be read, which the console routes later. An agent files nothing for a cluster it was not asked about, a proposed change is filed whole or not at all, and the same change for the same cases replaces the one waiting whatever its title.
- **A finding the system expects opens no case** (CF-82, CF-86): an agent marks a finding expected with its evidence, and it is shown on the run with its reason; an agent that makes up a validation rule says what it means.

#### Watson

- **Watson's console actions** (WC-12, ADR-101): adding, labelling and removing hosts outside Kubernetes (a host waits until a person pairs it), creating and changing fleets, and Automation variables and per-node values, each a SuperAdmin's to approve; a card never carries a host key or a credential.
- **Standing approvals and notifications** (WC-13 to WC-16, Settings › Watson): standing approvals for narrow classes outside production ("restart a crash-looping pod in development, at most 3 an hour"), and notifications of waiting proposed changes to a webhook, Slack or Microsoft Teams, with a summary and a link that opens the proposed change, never a secret or any output.

#### Mycroft's knowledge

- **Mycroft's wiki** (KN-01 to KN-12, KN-25 to KN-27, MY-01 to MY-03, CF-57, ADR-94 to ADR-96): a SuperAdmin connects sources in Settings › Knowledge (Confluence spaces, git repositories of runbooks, S3 buckets of documents, uploaded documents and the console itself), each tested in its dialog as typed and its credential kept in the Secret `<release>-knowledge`. Mycroft reads only what changed, scrubbed of secrets and reporting text that tries to give instructions, and writes wiki pages that name their sources, kept in git, an S3 bucket or, until one is set, a temporary ConfigMap. Each run's changes wait in the Inbox with every page's diff (two people for a critical page), and agents read only approved pages through `ask_knowledge`. AIOps › Mycroft › Wiki browses the pages, drafts and sources.
- **A Wiki sync job for each source** (KN-28, KN-29, ADR-141): Confluence every 15 minutes, git every hour and on each push, S3 and the console nightly, what people add when they add it; every sync is a run whose drawer says what was read, skipped and drafted. *Rebuild now…* reads a source whole again and redrafts its pages.
- **Added in Mycroft** (MY-04 to MY-08, MY-11): in Settings › Knowledge, an Admin or a SuperAdmin adds a note, a web address (its site's linked pages, robots.txt obeyed) or documents (PDF, Word, Markdown, HTML, text, CSV), which Mycroft writes up as pages for review.
- **This is outdated…** and **Retire page** on a wiki page (KN-23, KN-24, ADR-139): a correction becomes a source that wins over older ones and Mycroft redrafts the page with it; a page is retired, or made active again, on one person's request and another's approval in the Inbox, and agents no longer read it.
- **`ask_knowledge` knows the console's records** (KN-32, KN-33, CF-52, ADR-144): after the pages, the open known issues, the active fixes and, to who may read them, the active exceptions a question matches. Mycroft's drafts are given the fixes on their subject and flag a page that contradicts one (*Contradicts F-3, used in 4 cases*).
- **A proven fix becomes a runbook draft** (KN-34): the source *Fixes* offers Mycroft each active fix seen in three cases or more that no runbook draws on.
- **Inventory and drift** (GS-11, KN-19, KN-30, KN-31, ADR-98, ADR-143): the hosts a document lists that the console lacks, and the groups of hosts it names as fleets that would select nodes now, are proposed as changes (*3 nodes now: rke2cp1, rke2cp2, rke2cp3*); the drift check proposes each difference no known issue or exception covers, and the report lists what it left out and why.
- **Known issues in Confluence** (KN-13 to KN-18, ADR-97, Settings › Issues › Confluence): a Status page by service, a page per known issue and a resolved archive, kept current through an outbox; a Notes part people edit, and a person's edit of the rest waits for its owner. Reporters' names, report text, namespaces, hostnames and unapproved agent text never reach Confluence.

#### Reporting problems

- **Report a problem** (IS-01 to IS-28, CS-15 to CS-20, CF-63, CF-64, docs/issues.md): a page of its own at `/report`, outside the console's tabs, where people say what is wrong in their own words, with how bad it is and on which cluster if they know; a report that matches a known problem is linked to it at once with its workaround. Each report is a case in the Inbox, triaged by the people in `issues.operatorRoles` and investigated by the *Case triage* job; the reporter follows it under *My cases* and answers there. Ways in: *Report a problem* and *My cases* in the account menu, Sherlock's *Report this* and *Report this problem* on an error screen, their text handed over in the browser, never in the address. Systems file reports through `/api/issues/v1/` with API tokens. A fifth role, **Reporter** (`issues.reporterGroups`), sees the report page and nothing else.

#### Other areas

- **Hosts outside Kubernetes** (docs/automation.md): a SuperAdmin adds machines that are not nodes (*Add host…* on the Nodes tab), one or many at once, with their SSH port and how to pair them (an account's password or key, used once and kept nowhere, or a setup command run by hand, then *Test connection*); every host key is approved once by fingerprint. A paired host is a node for Automation (fleets, runs, facts, variables, reboot timeout), tagged *host* on the Nodes tab, never cordoned or drained. Kept under `ssh-hosts.json`; addresses inside `networkPolicy.nodeCIDRs`, other SSH ports through `networkPolicy.hostPorts`.
- **Scheduled backups to S3** (BK-04 to BK-14, ADR-81, ADR-82): Administration › Backup's *Scheduled backups to S3* card: bucket, prefix, region, an S3-compatible endpoint, an access key or the pod's AWS identity, a schedule in words, retention, SSE-S3 or SSE-KMS and optional encryption on the console under your passphrase; run on the leader, with *Test connection*, *Run now*, the last runs, and restores from the bucket. The console's own S3 client, no AWS SDK. Chart: `backup.s3.egressCIDRs`, `backup.s3.egressPorts`.
- **Getting started** (GS-01 to GS-12, ADR-103, ADR-104): a checklist for SuperAdmins in Administration › Settings and on the home page, nine steps from sign-in to a first backup, each checked against the console, never ticked by hand, with a link to where it is done. While a step is open, Sherlock's setup skill answers *What's next?* and never asks for a secret. See [docs/getting-started.md](docs/getting-started.md).
- **Charts from Helm repositories and OCI registries** (HR-01 to HR-12, ADR-123, ADR-124): Settings › Applications lists the repositories, each with *Test* and its credential in the Secret `<release>-helm-repositories`; *New application* takes a chart from git, a Helm repository or an OCI registry at a version; Watson takes `helm install NAME REPO/CHART --version V` and `oci://…`; a card deploys the archive it showed, by its digest. Chart: `helmRepositories.egressCIDRs`.

### Changed

- **Sherlock's settings are under Settings › AI**: its provider and key are the default provider's, its clusters, view control, model and instructions are in its drawer under Agents, and its caps are the overall limits. Its instructions of before, which replaced the built-in text, are carried over once as house instructions, which add to it. `/api/v1/sherlock/config` and `/api/v1/sherlock/key` keep working over the same data. Settings groups its sections under AI, Changes and AIOps.
- **Sherlock knows what Watson runs from Watson's own list** (AI-19): Sherlock and every agent know that Watson runs `helm install`, `upgrade` and `uninstall` and deletes a Secret or a namespace by name; the Troubleshooter checks its commands with `check_command`. Sherlock opens the Policies section it read (`drain`, `limits`, `priority`; AI-20), and the steps under its answers read as words (*Opened a tool dialog*; AI-21).
- **Automation's model calls are its agents'** (AI-18): drafting is the Automation author's, and every draft goes to the Script reviewer before you see it; a review is the Script reviewer's, and weighing a variable change the Change planner's.
- **Administration › Approvals is in AIOps › Inbox** (CS-06, IN-06), unchanged in what it lists and who approves; the old address leads there. The gear always opens Administration; its flag is gone, and the bell says what waits for you (CF-80).
- **One *Reject…* and *Withdraw…* dialog** (CF-102): a console change, a wiki page's request, a proposed change and a script's version ask the same way, with *Withdraw* for whoever asked.
- **Watson keeps only the newest request active** (CF-65): earlier ones fold into "N earlier requests left waiting", never discarded.
- **Backups** (BK-01 to BK-03, BK-15, ADR-80, ADR-137): *Download backup* saves `221b-backup-<time>.json.gz` (format `221b-backup/2`) with every part the console's packages register, the optional ones (chat conversations) when asked. Its secrets travel sealed (AES-256-GCM) with the install's key, kept in the Secret `<release>-backup-key` and never shown: restoring with the same key brings them back; with another key the console asks first, then lists every secret to set again and where. A restore brings back cluster connections and hosts outside Kubernetes that are missing, and reads the backups of before.
- **The Nodes page**: a box in the Node column's heading picks every node in view; *Collect facts* is on the bar over picked nodes and in a fleet's Tools, and says how many answered.
- **Automation's words** (review U-2, U-9): *Run profiles* and *Run history* in sentence case, and the script header taught as `# 221b:describe` (both forms still read); the Automation author is taught that form too (CF-106).
- **Words in the page's face** (CF-105): the values of select boxes, and a case's and a proposed change's title, are no longer monospace; that stays for ids, object names and commands.
- **AIOps reads cleanly** (CF-105): a case's rule, its first sighting, the job that found it and its resolution's due time are each on a line of their own (they ran together); Runs and a case name the agent and the cluster, not their ids; a rule's and a fix's state tags are capitalised; a long object in the Inbox ends in an ellipsis; the Validation rules page's lead is rewritten.
- **Close without validation** (CF-109): an agent-found case closed as Fixed waits for a run of its job to re-check the fix, and that run may never come (the alert no longer fires, the job is off). A box on the Fixed form, for one case and for several from the Inbox, closes it with no wait; *Skip validation…* does the same for a case already closed and waiting. No run then verifies or reopens it, and the problem found again opens a new case.
- **A case says what it waits for** (CF-107): beside its status, one tag in the Inbox and in the case's header says the step it is at: *Waiting for analysis*, *Analysis running*, *Fix suggested*, *Waiting for approval*, *Waiting for a second approval*, *Change running*, *Change failed*, *Waiting for a validation run*, *Waiting for closure* (the fix is in and a person closes it: verified at P0–P1, with the RCA, or applied to a person's case), *Fix did not hold*, and *Fix verified* on a case closed as Fixed. It replaces the *Analysis queued*, *fix applied*, *fix verified* and *Fix suggested* tags; a case closed as Fixed that still waits for its run now says so in the Inbox, and one a run reopened says *Fix did not hold*, in the Inbox and in its drawer. The API gives it as a case's `stage`. One validation run that re-checks the object and finds the problem gone is enough to close a case below P1.
- **The agents' and jobs' instructions after the review** (CF-106): one word, *validation rule*, for what a finding is named by; the audit's task no longer says it runs at night; the alert diagnosis is told how an alert's severity differs from a finding's; a re-check says plainly what *still* means; American spelling; and Sherlock's AIOps skill is in short sections.
- **A job's schedule also in your time** (CF-113): AIOps › Jobs gives a schedule in the console's zone and the runs in the browser's; where the two differ, the schedule's zone is followed by the same time in the browser's, "UTC · 6:00pm PST".

### Fixed

- **A console change waiting for a second SuperAdmin is applied once** when two approve at once; the second is told who approved it and when, as is a second approval of a Watson command (IB-09, ADR-111).
- **A change proposed again no longer loses its approvals** (CF-110, ADR-153): the same change an agent proposed again replaced the card that waited, and the card's approvals went with it. On a production cluster, where a change needs two people, the first approval was gone each time the nightly audit proposed the change again. The card that waits is now kept, with its approvals, and its history says *proposed again*; it is replaced only when what it acts on changed since.
- **A re-check is about the cluster the agent was asked about** (CF-108): in a run over several clusters, an answer about one could verify, or reopen, a fix on another by naming it; such an entry is now left out and the run says so. A re-check that names the cluster by its name, not its id, now counts.
- **A verified fix that is found again no longer reads as verified** (CF-108): at P0–P1 a case whose fix a run verified waits for its RCA; when a later run found the problem again it went on saying *Verified: add the RCA to close*. It now says the fix did not hold.
- **A case a re-check reopens is analyzed again** (CF-108): closed as Fixed and found still there by a run that re-checked it, it is queued for its analysis as one reopened by a finding is.
- **A page that fails to draw** shows an error screen with Reload and *Report this problem* instead of going blank (CS-19).
- **A run whose cases cannot be read says so** (CF-111): when the cases or the known issues could not be read, the agent was given the keys in use without them, as if no case were open and no fix waited, and the run said nothing. Now the run notes that its agent was not given the keys, as it does when the exceptions cannot be read.
- **A case names its job** (CF-112): a case's drawer showed the job's id ("found by Auditor (nightly-audit)"); it now shows its name, "Nightly audit", as the rest of the console does.
- **A case's drawer keeps its full height with the dock open** (CF-114): with Sherlock and Watson open in the dock, a case's drawer ended above the dock, a third shorter, with a second Sherlock and Watson under its own. The dock now steps aside while a drawer shows a Sherlock you can ask in, and comes back when it closes.
- **A waiting fix says why a run left it** (CF-115): when a run found a waiting fix's problem again but an exception, the agent's *expected* or the job's lowest priority kept it from opening a case, the case waited with nothing said and the run did not count it. It now counts as not re-checked, and the case says why ("run-12 found it again and opened no case: the agent marked it expected"); use *Skip validation* or reopen it.
- **A run routed twice no longer counts twice** (CF-116): a run whose findings were routed but which could not be saved was routed again on the next look, and every case it joined counted it twice (*seen ×N*), as did a known issue's *still present* and an exception's matches. Routing it again now changes nothing. A case is also seen once per run, however many of the run's findings name it.

### Added

- **Automation** (Phase 11 part 2, ADR-73, docs/automation.md). A new *Automation* tab for SuperAdmins: scripts with a `# 221b:` header, static checks, versions approved once (twice on a two-person cluster), fleet and node parameters, and runs on a fleet's paired nodes over SSH with dry run, batches, a failure limit, reboot and wait, cancel and per-node output. Scripts declaring `privilege none`, and every dry run, run as `nobody`. Needs `toolbox.ssh.enabled`; chart value `automation.enabled` (true) adds the ConfigMap `<release>-automation` (AU-01 to AU-14).
- **SSH to nodes** (Phase 11, ADR-72). A node's Tools menu gains *Pair for SSH…*, *SSH…* and *Unpair from SSH…* for SuperAdmins. Pairing is a node command that installs the console's certificate authority and a principals file for an existing login user on the node and pins the node's host key; a session is a terminal over SSH with a certificate the console signs for that session alone, checked against the pinned host key, and works when the kubelet does not. SSH is off until `toolbox.ssh.enabled` is set, and only then does the chart add an egress rule from the console to the nodes. Chart values `toolbox.ssh.enabled`, `toolbox.ssh.port` and `toolbox.ssh.loginUser`; the authority lives in the Secret `<release>-ssh-ca` (SSH-01 to SSH-07).
- **Administration › Audit log**: the newest 2,000 events the console recorded, who did what and when, searched by words and narrowed by action and outcome, with a JSON download. They are kept beside the settings (`audit-log.json` in the settings ConfigMap), so every replica and a restarted console show them; standard output and the audit webhook stay the full record (M2-01, ADR-71).
- **Administration › Backup**: one JSON file with everything the console keeps of its own (settings, local users, connections, Helm applications, Toolbox session records, Sherlock's spend, the audit log) and never a credential; and a restore of the parts picked from such a file, with a report of what is left to do by hand (M2-06, ADR-71).
- **Commands of a Helm application**: *New command…* in a release's Tools menu saves a named redeploy with its own `--set` parameters; the command is then an item of the menu, with Run, Edit and Delete (M2-07).
- The diff dialog compares any two of: a revision, the source at its deployed branch or tag, the source at any branch or tag (M2-05).
- Sherlock's question box recalls earlier questions with ↑ and ↓, as a shell does (M2-04). Watson's history, which ↑ walks, now holds every command the person ran there, a Redeploy or an application's command included, not only the typed ones.

## 0.9.1 (2026-10-27)

The first release since 0.7.1, with Phase 8 (Storage), Phase 9 (Toolbox) and the Phase 9.1 review; there is no 0.8.x or 0.9.0. [docs/upgrading.md](https://github.com/ucesys/221b/blob/v0.9.1/docs/upgrading.md#091) says what an upgrade from 0.7.1 changes.

### Added

- **Toolbox.** A Tools button beside each pod and node opens a shell, forwards a port to your computer, adds a debug container, starts a debug pod, or opens a shell on a node, and cordons, drains or deletes. Each tool asks through a dialog, and its button opens the session as a tab in the dock, at once: it runs as the person who asked, and ends after 10 minutes without activity, at its limit (Extend adds 10 minutes, up to the console's maximum), or when its pod is gone. Cordon runs at once; Drain and Delete ask first; Watson shows them running. On production where the two-person rule is on, another SuperAdmin approves through Watson, since the session opens as the person who asked, with their rights; node shells follow the same rule (`toolbox.nodeShell.approval: always` brings back a second SuperAdmin everywhere). Pods on a node that stopped reporting show Unknown, in a status segment of their own; the Pods segments show only when they have pods. A pod raises a Pending alert only after a grace period, 2 minutes by default, set on the Settings page. Port forwards reach your computer through the new `221b` command, attached to each release; it asks for the forward's token, so the token stays out of your shell's history. Administration gains Sessions (open and ended) and Toolbox (what the chart allows). Sherlock can open a tool's dialog filled in. A debug pod carries no ServiceAccount token unless a SuperAdmin names the account. Chart: a `toolbox` block, `rbac.roles.admin.exec`, and the `221b-toolbox` namespace for node shells; the full Admin role gains port forwards and Jobs, which the Permissions page flags `runs pods`, since a Job may run as any ServiceAccount of its namespace.
- **Storage tab.** Storage classes, persistent volumes and claims, and the pods that mount them, in one table beside a filter sidebar by namespace, node and storage class, as on the Pods tab, with how full each volume is (from the kubelets), a Needs attention filter, and a drawer that traces a claim from its class to its node and says where it breaks. A class has a drawer of its own. Expand a claim, delete a claim, and set or remove the default class through Watson, each after an approval. People bound to namespaces see their claims only. Alerts, the pod drawer and the banner link to it. Full Admin gains `delete` on claims, and the chart's ClusterRole reads VolumeAttachments.
- **Fleets by name.** A fleet can now be a list of nodes kept in 221B, as well as a set of labels and taints. A list changes nothing in the cluster, a node may be on any number of lists, and a new node joins one only when someone adds it; the editor offers both kinds.
- **Fleets: pick nodes and put them in a fleet.** Administrators tick nodes in the matrix, then make a new fleet of them (a list, by default), add them to a fleet or remove them from one, choosing the fleet in the dialog; Cancel drops the picks. For a fleet of labels and taints the dialog lists the `kubectl label` and `kubectl taint` commands, and what else they change (fleets the nodes leave, pods a taint keeps off or evicts), and sends them to Watson, where each waits for approval.
- Watson runs `kubectl taint` on nodes. Its card says what changes and, for NoExecute, which pods are evicted.
- **Alerts by node.** Each pod's alert sits under the node it runs on, below the node's own alert when it has one, and a node whose pods fail comes near the top even when the node itself is fine. Pods that no node took and volumes keep their own sections; By kind shows the alerts as before.

### Changed

- **Tabs open as they were left.** A tab in the bar opens with the filters, selection and sort it had, per cluster, for the rest of the session; a details pane over it is not kept.

- **Sherlock knows the cluster it is asked about.** Its prompt now names the cluster's Kubernetes distribution (RKE2, K3s, kubeadm, kind, EKS, GKE, AKS, OpenShift, Talos), the operating system and kernel of each node, and its network plugin, load balancer, storage and GPU plugin, and gives the guidance for those alone: the distribution's own services, log files and commands, and each operating system's. It troubleshoots down to the node's operating system and hardware, and gives the checks to run on a node in a block of their own. A console with several clusters gives each its own prompt. Node details are included only for people who may list nodes.
- **Namespaces: a tab per kind.** A namespace's kinds are tabs with their counts, as on the Helm tab, Pods first and Helm releases last, in place of a table per kind one under another and the kinds funnel.
- **Alert banners can be hidden.** Settings › General › Alert banners: Hide takes the alert banners off the top of every tab, for everyone; the Alerts tab's name still turns red or amber.
- **Home: figures only, and Storage.** A cluster card's tiles show only their figure: nodes ready, pods ready of those not finished (94/104; completed pods are left out), alerts, and a new Storage tile, claims bound, in place of Namespaces. The health lines no longer say that a node not ready also has errors, and call the pods on a node that stopped reporting Unknown rather than failing to start.
- **Home: Troubleshoot or Audit.** A cluster card's Sherlock button reads Troubleshoot while the cluster has errors or warnings, and asks Sherlock why and how to fix each, most severe first; while it is healthy it reads Audit and asks for the review of its setup, as before.
- Sherlock's prompt is cached for longer: the time comes last, so asking again a minute later costs less.
- Chart: `antiAffinity` (`soft` or `hard`, off by default) keeps several replicas on different nodes: soft prefers it more strongly than the spread the chart already asks for, hard requires it.

- **Nodes and Fleets: All, With problems, With errors.** The Nodes with errors checkbox is now a switch of three: every node, the nodes with an error or a warning (cordoned, under pressure, pods not ready), or only those with an error. Links with err=1 still show the nodes with errors.

### Fixed

- **The Tools menu and its confirmations had no background.** They are drawn outside the table, and took the theme's colours with them only on the page itself.
- **A bound volume's drawer said its claim was none.** It now shows the claim's chain, with the claim as a link back to its drawer; the Volume link in the Storage table opens the volume's drawer and the Claim link the claim's, where both used to open the claim's.
- The filter sidebars of the Pods and Storage tabs no longer show an "only" button on hover; the checkboxes and Select all do the same.
- **The home page counted a cordoned node as not ready.**
- **An acknowledgement took a second or two to show.** It is saved with the settings, and the replica serving the page, when it is not the one that saved it, reads them every 3 seconds. The Alerts tab, its badge and its colour now show it at once, and the server's rows take over when they come.
- **The home page's pod counts did not add up**, and differed from the Pods tab's: "109 pods · 106 running · 10 not ready" counted the not-ready pods among the running ones. The tile now reads "96 running · 10 not ready · 3 completed", and the Pods and Helm tabs have a Not ready status beside Running: pods running but failing a readiness check, or on a node that stopped reporting. The Nodes, Pods and Alerts boxes stay plain when nothing is wrong.
- **Copy buttons copied nothing** when the console is reached over plain HTTP, as at http://<address>: browsers offer their clipboard API only to HTTPS and localhost. Copying now falls back to the older way, which works there too.
- The refresh ring kept the old pace after the refresh rate changed, until the next refresh. It now starts again at the new rate at once, as the server does.
- **"Show me the nodes with problems" showed every node.** Sherlock could not set the Nodes and Fleets tabs' filter or the Alerts tab's Hide acknowledged, so it guessed at taints and search words and then cleared the filters; and with only an errors filter, a node with warnings alone left it no filter that fit. It now shows the nodes with problems, or with errors, as asked, leaves a filtered view empty when nothing matches, and cannot take off a filter it set while the filter shows something.
